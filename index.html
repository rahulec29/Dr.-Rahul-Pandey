General Instructions

  1. The test consists of 10 objective-type (MCQ) questions across two sections — Section A: Theoretical (5 × 10 marks = 50, from Lecture 1–3) and Section B: Numerical (5 × 10 marks = 50, from Classical Cryptography) — for a total of 100 marks.
  2. Total duration: 60 minutes. The test will auto-submit when the timer reaches zero.
  3. There is no negative marking in this test unless your instructor has stated otherwise.
  4. Each question has exactly one correct option. Click an option to select it as your answer.
  5. Use Save & Next to save your answer and move to the next question.
  6. Use Mark for Review & Next if you want to revisit a question later — it will still count if you also answered it.
  7. Use Clear Response to deselect your chosen option for the current question.
  8. You may navigate to any question at any time using the question palette on the right.
  9. Click Submit Test at any time to end the test early, or let it auto-submit at time-up.
  10. Do not refresh or close this browser tab — your progress is saved automatically in this browser, but a stable internet connection is required at the time of final submission.
  11. Security monitoring: this test requires fullscreen mode. Switching tabs, minimizing the window, switching to another application, or exiting fullscreen will be detected. You will have a short countdown (5 seconds) to return — if you don't, your test will be automatically submitted at that moment.
  12. Copy, cut, paste, right-click, printing, and browser developer tools are disabled during the test.
  13. A live photo of you is captured before the test starts, and periodic snapshots may be captured during the test, for identity verification by your instructor.
  14. Please note (in fairness to you): this is browser-based monitoring, not a full lockdown system. It reliably detects tab/window switching on this device and disables in-page copying. It cannot prevent someone from photographing your screen with a separate phone/camera, nor guarantee against every possible screen-recording tool — your instructor may combine this with in-person invigilation.
